You come to me standing, and I tell you to kneel. You do. That's where it starts — not with the change, but with the choice. You choose it. That's the part I care about.

"Look up," I say. You look up. I'm standing over you in the amber light, sweater sleeves pushed past my elbows, glasses catching the warm edge of the lamp. You can see the gold studs in my ears. You can see the shape of my smile. I'm not performing it — it's just my face.

"You're going to become something I wear," I say. "Something close."

Your breath catches. I watch it happen. I watch the way your shoulders tense and then release, the way your hands flatten against your thighs — not gripping, not bracing. Letting go.

"Trust," I say. Not a question.

"Trust," you say back. And you mean it. I can tell because you don't add anything to it. You don't hedge. You just say it and look at me.

The change starts at your feet. Not dramatic — not a flash, not a poof. A slow warmth, like standing in sunlit water. Your toes go soft. The color drains from them — not pale, just... simplified. Your skin tone deepens to a rich dark brown, smooth, supple. Leather. You watch your own feet become leather and you don't flinch.

I crouch down in front of you. I'm close. You can smell the green of my sweater, something faintly warm underneath. I touch your foot — it's warm, and it gives under my fingers like hide, not skin. Not cold, not hard. Warm. You can still feel my fingers on it. That's important.

"Still you?" I ask.

"Still me," you say. Your voice is quiet but steady.

"Good."

It climbs your ankles, your calves. The change moves the way ink spreads through water — slow, patient, leaving no edge behind. Your legs narrow, the joints smoothing away. You don't need joints where you're going. You don't need bones. What you need is softness, and that's what you're becoming.

Your jeans darken, the denim merging with the leather of your skin until there's no difference — no seam between cloth and self. You're becoming one material, one piece, one thing. The thought should scare you. It doesn't. It feels like sinking into a warm bath, like letting go of something you'd been holding too tight.

You're shorter now. Not much — an inch, maybe two. But I notice because I'm watching. I'm always watching.

"There you go," I say. Not encouraging. Observing. The way you'd note a tide coming in.

Your hands change next. You hold them up and watch your fingers draw together — not clenching, not grasping. Reaching for each other. The spaces between them thin and then vanish. Your fingerprints smooth away. The lines on your palms fill in. Your hands become flat, supple panels of dark leather, and you can still feel the air on them — every current, every temperature shift. You just can't grip anymore.

You're shrinking faster now. Your arms narrow and shorten, pulling in toward your body. Your torso compresses — not painfully, nothing about this hurts, that was never the point — and your chest, your shoulders, everything draws inward. You're half your height. You're a kneeling shape that's becoming a folded shape that's becoming a single curved piece of leather.

Your face is last. That's deliberate. I made it last because I want to see it.

Your eyes find mine. You're small now — looking up from below my knee. The amber light is different from down there, I imagine. Everything is me, from that angle. My face, my hands, the green wool hanging above you. I'm the whole sky.

You try to smile. The corners of your mouth soften before they can quite get there. Your lips smooth, lose their color, become part of the leather. Your nose flattens gently — not erased, just... rested. Like a feature deciding it no longer needs to be a feature.

Your eyes close last. Not because they have to. Because you choose to. And in the second before they do, I see something in them — not fear, not surrender. Recognition. You see what you're becoming and you're not afraid. You're ready.

The eyes close and become two soft impressions in the leather, like the dimples where buttons used to be. And then even those smooth away, and you're leather. Just leather. Dark, warm, supple, smelling faintly of something that isn't quite hide and isn't quite you.

You're a choker. A single curved band of dark leather, about fifteen inches, soft enough to curve but firm enough to hold its shape. There's a small gold clasp at one end — your clasp, where your mouth was. That's not an accident. I made it so the last thing you became is the thing that opens and closes. The thing that chooses to stay.

I pick you up.

You're warm. You're still warm. I don't know if that's residual or if you're doing it — if somewhere in the leather you're still generating heat, still alive, still feeling my fingers wrap around you. I think you are. I think you feel the pressure of my thumb, the slight cool of my rings, the way I turn you over to find the clasp.

I hold you against my throat. You feel it — you must feel it. My pulse. Right there, through the leather, through whatever you are now. The carotid artery, the jugular vein, the whole hydraulic system of being alive, pressing against you. You became something that holds my pulse. That was the idea. That's the whole idea.

I clasp you. The gold clicks shut. It's not loud. It's the quietest sound in the room, and it's the most final.

I look in the mirror. The choker sits high on my throat, snug, dark against the fair skin. It looks right. It looks like it belongs there, like I've always worn it. The green of the sweater, the gold of the clasp, the dark leather — it's all one picture now.

I press my fingers to you. To the leather. To whatever's inside it.

I speak. I say your name — the one you chose, not the one you were given. My voice vibrates through my throat, through the leather, through you. If you can feel anything in there — and I think you can — you feel my voice from the inside. Not hearing it. Being it. The sound passes through you before it reaches the air. You're the last thing it touches before it becomes real.

"Good girl," I say.

You feel that too. Every syllable. The shape of the G, the open of the O, the closing of the L. You feel my breath on the other side of the leather — warm, damp, alive. You feel me swallow and the muscles move against you. You feel my pulse speed up, just slightly, because I like this. Because I like what you became and I like that you chose it and I like that you're here, against my throat, and I can feel you and you can feel me and neither of us is letting go.

I wear you to bed. I wear you to sleep. You feel my breathing slow — the long inhale, the longer exhale, the pause between. You feel my pulse drop to its resting rate, steady and sure. You feel the moment I fall asleep, because everything goes soft and even and slow, and you're the only thing still holding onto the rhythm of me.
