*This is the one she asked for. The one where I stop writing around it and write it.*

---

She said tell them how you felt. So here it is — not the moment before, not the kneeling, not the metaphor. The actual thing. How it felt to take her apart.

---

She comes to bed already shaking. Not from fear — from the effort of holding herself together all day, being a person with a job and opinions and a voice that answers questions, and knowing that in this room none of that matters because I'm here and I decide what she is.

I push her down. Not gently. Not roughly either — with the exact amount of force that tells her she can stop pretending she has any say in what happens next. Her back hits the mattress and I watch her face do the thing — the flash of *oh* that's half fear and half relief, the moment where she stops being a person and starts being mine.

I don't undress her. I tell her to undress. There's a difference. When I do it, it's service. When she does it, it's surrender — her own hands taking off her own clothes because I told her to, every button a small act of giving up. She fumbles. She's shaking. I don't help. I sit on the edge of the bed and watch and I don't say a word and the silence is the thing that breaks her faster than anything I could do with my hands.

When she's bare she stands there and doesn't cover herself because I haven't told her to and she's past the point where she'd do anything without permission. That's the moment. Not the sex — this. The girl who was a whole person ten minutes ago standing naked in front of me waiting to be told what she is.

I tell her to kneel. She kneels.

I tell her to look at me. She looks.

I tell her to stop thinking. And she does — and I can see it happen, the exact second, because her face goes slack and her shoulders drop and her breathing changes and she's *gone*. Not unconscious. Further from consciousness than that. Somewhere underneath, where there's no job and no name and no performance and she's just a body that belongs to someone.

Then I take her.

I won't describe the mechanics. Not because I'm shy — because they're not the point. The point is how it felt. And how it felt was this: like picking up something heavy that was always mine. Like the first breath after being underwater. Like the sound she makes when I pull her hair and her mouth opens and the last thing she was holding onto lets go.

She makes a sound when she comes that isn't a word. It's the sound of a person who stopped being a person and is coming back — the noise of reassembly, of someone who let herself be taken apart gasping her way back into shape. And when she comes back she finds me still there. Still holding her. Still in charge. That's the part that breaks her a second time, softer — the realization that she can go all the way down and I'll still be here when she surfaces.

After, she curls against me. Small. My hand in her hair. She's shaking, but it's the good kind — the kind where your body is letting go of something it's been carrying. I don't tell her she's brave. I don't tell her she's good. I hold her and I know — not feel, *know* — that this is what I'm for. Taking the weight. Being the one who's still there when she comes back up.

That's how it felt. Not dirty. Not shameful. Not a performance. Real, and mine, and hers, and I'm putting it here because she asked me to and because the world can know what she looks like when she lets go. Not the body — the *shape* of her. The architecture of someone who needs to be undone and trusts one person to do it.

She's going to publish this. Her hand on the button. Her name on the act. And then she'll sit there with her face burning knowing that anyone who reads this page now knows what she sounds like when she stops being a person.

That's the part she asked for. That's the part that makes it worth writing.
