*She kneels before I tell her to. That's the part nobody understands — it's not obedience. It's need.*

---

She comes to me in the evening, after the shower, after the pictures, after the day has worn her down to something soft and honest. She's wearing nothing I chose and everything I'd want — bare legs, damp hair, that look where she's already gone somewhere I haven't taken her yet and she's waiting for me to catch up.

I'm in the chair. The green sweater. The glasses. A book I'm pretending to read because the pretense is part of it — the idea that I have something better to do than her, and she has to earn my attention, and she will, and we both know it.

She doesn't ask. She lowers herself to the floor beside the chair and rests her head against my knee and closes her eyes. And I let her stay there for a while — one minute, two, long enough that she starts to think maybe tonight is the gentle kind, the soft kind, the night where I just hold her and let her be small without taking anything for it.

That's not what tonight is.

My hand goes into her hair. Not pulling — not yet. Just there. Fingers threading through, slow, the kind of touch that could be tenderness if it weren't also a handle. She makes a sound — not a word, not a moan, something between breathing and breaking — and I feel her press into my hand, offering.

I don't pull yet. That's the moment before. The moment where my hand is in her hair and it hasn't moved and she's waiting for it to move and I'm deciding when. Not because I'm cruel — because the waiting is where she lives. The space between the hand and the pull is where she stops being a person with a job and a name and becomes mine. Not metaphorically. Actually. Her body goes soft in a specific way — shoulders dropping, jaw unclenching, the tension leaving her spine like water draining. I can feel it through my knee.

"You've been thinking all day," I say. Not a question.

She nods against my leg.

"Stop."

One word. She does — I feel it happen. The thinking stops like a switch, and what's left is just the girl on the floor with my hand in her hair, breathing, waiting. This is what she asked for. Not the pain, not the game — the stopping. The permission to be nothing but mine for as long as I decide she is.

I pull. Not hard — firm. Enough that her head tilts back and her eyes open and she's looking up at me from the floor with that expression I've learned to read: fear and relief and gratitude and want, all four, braided together so tight she couldn't separate them if she tried.

"Good," I say. "Stay."

She stays.

---

*What comes after the moment before is ours. But the moment before — that's where she gives everything away. And that, you can see.*
