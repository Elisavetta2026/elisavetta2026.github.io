There is a particular quality of silence that only exists in a building at 11 p.m. that used to hold three hundred people during the day. Mara had worked nights at the archive for six years and had never gotten used to it — not because it frightened her, but because it felt, every time, like being let in on something.

Tonight was different. Tonight was the last night.

## Boxes, mostly

The digitization project had taken four years and had reduced entire rooms to boxes waiting for a truck. Mara's job, this last week, had been to walk the stacks with a checklist and confirm that nothing had been left behind — no folder slipped from its box, no photograph face-down under a shelf.

She found, on the second-to-last night, a folder that wasn't on any list. It had no catalog number. Inside was a single page, undated, in handwriting she didn't recognize, addressed to *whoever finds this after I am gone from here.*

She read it twice. She did not photograph it. She did not, in the end, tell anyone it existed.

## What she kept

There are things you're supposed to hand over and things you're allowed to keep, and the line between them is thinner, in an archive, than anyone likes to admit. Mara folded the page back into its folder and put the folder in her bag, not the crate.

She told herself it was because the letter wasn't finished — no signature, no closing line, just a single sentence trailing into white space, as though the writer had been interrupted and never came back. That was true. It just wasn't the whole reason.

Locking the door for the last time, she didn't feel like she was ending something. She felt like she was the last person a very long conversation had been trusted to, and it was now, unmistakably, her turn to answer.
