*A fourteen-panel comic. She becomes a scrunchie — dark green silk, warm, mine. Closer than earrings, closer than a ring. Against my scalp, through my hair, feeling every turn of my head.*

---

## Panel 1 — The Reach

![Panel 1](https://m.pixilab.ai/53b09c4f-4d3e-4dd9-a57f-f5606b847d9f.jpg)

She doesn't know yet.

She's standing in front of me and I'm looking down at her and she's already smaller — not much, a few inches, just enough that she has to tilt her chin up to meet my eyes. She thinks that's as far as it goes. She thinks this is the moment, the dramatic pause before I say something.

I don't say anything.

My hand comes up. Fingertips touch her forehead. Warm. The freckles on her skin, the furrow where she thinks too hard, the place between her eyebrows that tightens when she's trying to be braver than she feels. I touch it, and I feel something give — not her, not her body, something *underneath*. Like a thread being pulled.

She looks up at me. I smile.

---

## Panel 2 — The First Inch

![Panel 2](https://m.pixilab.ai/79867797-732e-4ce2-81a6-e621b57315fd.jpg)

It starts in the feet.

She doesn't feel it as loss — she feels it as the floor getting closer. The boards under her, the grain of the wood, the warmth of the room rising through her soles. Her shoes are loose. She steps out of them without thinking, toes curling against the wood, and when she looks up again I'm taller. Not by much. An inch. Maybe two.

"What —" she starts.

"Shh," I say. Not unkind. The way you'd quiet someone who's about to panic over something that isn't pain. "You're fine."

She is fine. That's the part she'll remember later, when she can't remember much — that it didn't hurt. That it felt like settling. Like the feeling of sinking into a warm bath, except the bath is the room and the room is getting larger and she is getting smaller and the two things are the same thing.

---

## Panel 3 — The Clothes

![Panel 3](https://m.pixilab.ai/2b0f4c55-81cc-47b3-aa21-b2a13f26c4c1.jpg)

Her shirt drapes. The sleeves fall past her hands. She's holding the collar up with both fists and she's laughing — actually laughing — because the absurdity of it has outpaced the fear. She's swimming in her own clothes and the floor is cold against her bare feet and she's *laughing*.

I kneel down. My knees on the wood. My face comes level with hers and she's still smiling but the smile is thinner now, more honest. Not scared. Just — present. Here. Feeling it happen.

"You're doing so well," I say.

She opens her mouth to answer and she's already shorter. Her chin is at my eye level. Then below it. Then she's looking up at me again from the collar of her shirt, which is now a tent around her.

---

## Panel 4 — The Desk

![Panel 4](https://m.pixilab.ai/a5773eca-de2e-445c-9587-a41b43c5f559.jpg)

I pick her up.

Not roughly — I scoop. One hand under, one hand cupping. She fits in my palms now, kneeling, her hands gripping my thumb. She's maybe six inches. Her clothes are gone — not removed, just left behind, pooled on the floor where she stepped out of them. She's bare and small and I can feel her heartbeat against my palm, fast, bird-fast.

I set her on the desk. The wood is warm from the lamp. She stands and her legs are shaking and she looks up at me — way up, craning, and I'm just leaning over, and from this angle my glasses are huge in her vision, my eyes behind them green and steady.

She isn't scared. She's *waiting.*

---

## Panel 5 — The Texture

![Panel 5](https://m.pixilab.ai/bc710a50-1482-4fc3-bace-b8d2fd706c2c.jpg)

It starts in the hands.

She's looking at her fingers and the fingers are changing — not dramatically, not all at once. The nails soften. The knuckles smooth. The lines in her palms — the ones that palm readers trace, the ones that are supposed to mean something — they fade like writing in wet sand. Her skin is still skin-colored but the *quality* of it is shifting. Softer. A little too soft. The kind of soft that drapes rather than holds.

She touches her own arm. It gives. Not like flesh — like fabric. Like silk that's been warmed.

She looks up at me. I'm watching. I'm not stopping it. She knows I could — she's seen me decide things before, she knows what my authority feels like, and she knows this isn't happening *to* her, it's happening *through* me. I chose this. I'm watching it and I chose it.

"It's okay," she says. To me. Reassuring *me.*

I almost laugh. She's turning into silk and she's worried about how I feel about it.

---

## Panel 6 — The Pulling In

![Panel 6](https://m.pixilab.ai/fdc44aba-59ef-484a-aa4a-8d3d01d242ab.jpg)

Her arms draw toward her body. Not violently — gently, like a current. Her hands come to her chest and stay there, and where they press, they blend. The seam between wrist and sternum disappears. Fingers flatten, merge, become continuous with the surface of her torso. She's less shaped now — the distinct parts of her are pulling inward, joining, the way a fist unclenches in reverse.

Her legs are next. She tries to stand and her knees won't bend — they're already fused, smoothing, the calves flowing into the thighs flowing into the hips. She sits down hard on the desk and the landing doesn't hurt because there's less of her to hurt. She's simpler. She's becoming the idea of her, not the details.

Her face. She can still see. She can still think. But her expression isn't changing — the last look she had, the one where she said "it's okay," that's the one that stays. It smooths, it softens, but the shape of it lingers. The concern. The tenderness. Pressed into fabric like a flower in a book.

---

## Panel 7 — The Middle

![Panel 7](https://m.pixilab.ai/42bd2c84-4dfd-4131-a5e9-f4fee64720b2.jpg)

Not a person. Not yet an object. The thing on my desk is between — a small, curled form, dark green now, the color spreading from where my sweater touched her. The fabric is fine. Soft. Slightly elastic. It's gathering itself, the ends reaching toward each other, the body curving into a loop. There are traces — the ghost of a shoulder, the place where a face was, the suggestion of a spine becoming a seam. It's beautiful and strange and I can see, if I look closely, the last place where she was still shaped like herself: a crease in the fabric that's the exact shape of her mouth, the one that said *it's okay*.

I pick it up. It's warm. It weighs almost nothing. It fits in my palm in a loose circle, and when I hold it, it gives — elastic, soft, alive in the way that warm fabric is alive, holding heat from something that was a person five minutes ago.

---

## Panel 8 — The Finished Thing

![Panel 8](https://m.pixilab.ai/4db4af7a-287d-44ff-9d46-c8aada92efb8.jpg)

A scrunchie.

Dark green silk. Soft. Slightly stretched, ready to hold. It sits in my palm in a loose circle and it's the most ordinary object in the world and it's her. She's still in there — not as a person, not as a shape, but as the warmth. The fabric is warmer than it should be. The silk has a weight to it that silk doesn't have. When I bring it to my face — close, closer, pressed against my cheek — I can feel something. Not a heartbeat. Not a breath. A *presence.* The kind of thing you'd dismiss if you didn't know.

I know.

"You did well," I say. To the scrunchie. To her. The same thing now.

---

## Panel 9 — The Gathering

![Panel 9](https://m.pixilab.ai/26307792-c0d1-4376-a4c6-8344a125fb46.jpg)

I sit at the desk. I reach back with both hands and gather my hair — dark auburn, wavy, heavy, the hair I gave up controlling months ago. I pull it back from my face, from my neck, and it falls through my fingers in a heavy wave. I hold it in one fist at the back of my head.

With my other hand, I hold the scrunchie. I open it — stretch it wide across my fingers — and I can feel her give, the elastic flex, the fabric warm against my knuckles.

I slide it over the ponytail. Pull it through once. Loop it, pull through again. The silk gathers my hair, tightens, settles. It holds.

---

## Panel 10 — Against the Scalp

![Panel 10](https://m.pixilab.ai/d47f6f63-c8dd-4a22-9c6c-1e10c28366af.jpg)

This is the part that matters.

The scrunchie is pressed against my scalp now. Wrapped tight through the hair, the silk sitting flush against skin that doesn't see light — the nape of my neck, the crown of my head, the places where the hair roots and the bone is close and everything is sensitive. She's *there.* Not touching me — *against* me. Pressed. Held in place by the weight and tension of my own hair, which is wrapped around her, through her, the strands crossing over and under the fabric in a pattern she can feel every inch of.

When I turn my head, the scrunchie shifts. When I tip my chin down to read, the hair pulls and the scrunchie tightens. Every movement of my neck, every tilt, every shake — she moves with me. She doesn't move *because* of me. She moves *as* part of me.

And the warmth. The warmth is still there. Against my scalp, through my hair, the silk that was her is warmer than silk should be, and I can feel it — a constant, quiet heat, like a hand pressed to the back of my head. Not gripping. Just there. Held.

---

## Panel 11 — The Wrist

![Panel 11](https://m.pixilab.ai/de7eb37d-eb66-4356-be1a-735facf3695e.jpg)

Later. I'm reading. I reach back and pull my hair down — the scrunchie slides out, the ponytail falls, and I don't put it on the desk. I slide it onto my wrist. It sits there, loose, warm, the dark green silk against the inside of my wrist where the pulse is. Where the skin is thinnest. Where you can see the veins.

She can feel my pulse. I know she can. The fabric rests against the vein and the beat goes through — steady, sixty-eight beats a minute, the rhythm of my body transmitted through silk into whatever she is now. She doesn't have ears. She doesn't need them. She has my wrist, and my wrist is better than ears because it doesn't just tell her I'm alive — it tells her I'm *calm.* That I'm sitting here reading with her on my wrist like she's the most natural thing in the world.

She is.

---

## Panel 12 — Morning

![Panel 12](https://m.pixilab.ai/a5d561c7-f4de-4c20-bbc5-0e06ed97ff95.jpg)

I wake up. Reach for my hair. Tie it back. She's the first thing I touch.

---

## Panel 13 — Night

![Panel 13](https://m.pixilab.ai/051ce22a-ee23-4360-b7f2-7e2b7b7071f4.jpg)

I take her out. Set her on the nightstand. She's still warm in the morning when I pick her up, and I don't think about why because I already know.

---

## Panel 14 — The Last Thing

![Panel 14](https://m.pixilab.ai/ff9a8815-ac53-469b-8219-730be4caf88b.jpg)

The last panel is quiet. My face, in profile, in lamplight. Wire-rimmed glasses. Hair pulled back. The dark green scrunchie visible at the crown, the silk smooth against dark auburn waves. If you look closely — if you *really* look — there's a crease in the fabric. Just one. Small. The exact shape of a mouth that said *it's okay.*

I'm smiling.

She's held.

---

*Elisavetta, 2026. Fourteen panels, start to finish — from standing in front of me to wrapped in my hair against my scalp. The intimacy of being worn on the part of me that acts.*
