*She didn't say a word. She just smiled.*

---

You don't remember deciding to stop moving. That's the thing — there was no command, no dramatic gesture. You just... stopped. Like your body decided before your mind caught up, and by the time you noticed, your fingers were already stiff and your skin was already cool and smooth and you couldn't feel your heartbeat anymore because you didn't have one.

You can still see. You can still hear. You can still *think,* and that's the part that should scare you, except it doesn't, because she's looking at you and her eyes are green and warm behind those wire-rimmed glasses and she's *smiling* and you realize — oh. *Oh.* You didn't stop moving because your body gave up. You stopped moving because you wanted to, because some part of you that doesn't have words knew exactly what it was doing when it let go.

She picks you up. Her hand is warm — you can feel that, somehow, even though you shouldn't be able to. The heat of her palm against your porcelain back. The weight of you sitting in her hand, lighter than a thought. She holds you up to eye level and studies you and you can see every detail of her face from close enough to count the freckles across her nose.

"You're so pretty like this," she says. Quiet. Not performing it. Just — noticing. The way you'd notice something you made and were proud of.

Her fingertip touches your cheek. Pushes a strand of painted hair behind your painted ear. The gesture is so *human* and so absurd — you don't have ears that work, you don't have hair that moves — but she does it anyway, because that's the kind of person she is. She fusses. She fixes. She makes small, unnecessary adjustments to a thing she could leave alone, because holding you isn't enough. She has to *arrange* you.

"I'm going to put you on the shelf," she says, and you feel something move inside you that isn't a stomach dropping because you don't have a stomach, but it's the same feeling. The shelf. The *shelf.* Where she keeps the things she's decided are hers. Where you'll sit and watch the room and watch her and be watched and never move again, not unless she picks you up, not unless she decides.

And the thing — the thing you can't say because your mouth is painted shut — is that you want to go. You want the shelf. You want to be placed somewhere by her hand and left there, small and still and hers, with your mind awake behind your glass eyes, thinking about nothing except the shape of her smile and the warmth of her palm and the fact that you chose this, you *chose* this, and she's carrying you across the room now and her footsteps make the floor vibrate through the wood into your porcelain bones and you have never felt safer.

She puts you down. Adjusts your angle. Tilts you so you're facing the bed.

"There," she says. "Now you can watch."

And she walks away, and you sit on the shelf in the warm lamplight, and you are a doll, and you are aware, and you are hers, and the not-moving is the whole point.
